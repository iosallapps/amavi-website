1. Who we are

Visto Apps is the brand of Darius Cirjan, the developer and data controller. This policy explains what the Amavi app (iOS) and the website https://amaviapp.com do with personal data.

Contact for any privacy question or request: iosallapps@gmail.com. Website: vistoapps.com. We answer within one month (art. 12 GDPR). We are not required to appoint a Data Protection Officer.

Last updated: November 15, 2026.

2. At a glance

  • Your cycle and health data stay on your iPhone. Period days, flow, symptoms, mood, energy, sleep, discharge, sexual activity, temperature, weight, medication, pregnancy and ovulation tests, birth control and notes are stored only on your device. We have no server that receives them, and the app contains no analytics and no crash-reporting service.
  • No account. Amavi does not ask for your email or phone number. Your first name is optional.
  • Amavi is free and shows an ad from Google AdMob when you open the app or come back to it. What you log is never put into an ad request. Google still receives the technical data any ad request involves (see the table). Personalised ads only with your consent; in the EEA, UK and Switzerland no ad is requested before you answer Google's consent form.
  • Apple Health sync is optional and off until you turn it on. It runs on your iPhone; nothing goes to us.
  • Amavi has no passcode or Face ID lock of its own, and no export button. To remove everything, use Profile, Delete all data.
  • We do not sell personal data. Health-specific rules for US residents are in the section United States, under Consumer health data.

3. Data that stays on your device

These stay only in Amavi's storage on your iPhone:

  • Your logs: period days and flow, spotting, symptoms, mood, energy, sleep, discharge, sexual activity, temperature, weight, medication, pill taken, habits, ovulation and pregnancy test results, postpartum bleeding and free-text notes, kept in an on-device database (SwiftData).
  • Your profile and settings: your goal, birth date, region, units, cycle and period length, optional first name, pregnancy or postpartum details, birth control plan, tracking preferences, reminder choices and Apple Health sync setting.
  • A small widget snapshot in an App Group shared with Amavi's own widgets: your cycle day and days until your next period for the next five weeks, or your due date or baby's birth date in pregnancy and postpartum modes. Not your journal. If you add a widget to your Lock Screen, anyone who can see the Lock Screen can see it.
  • Amavi AI. Your questions and the context Amavi gives the assistant (a summary of what you logged) are processed on your iPhone by Apple's on-device model, or answered from texts in the app. The conversation is kept only while the app is running and is not saved.
  • Reminders are local notifications scheduled by your iPhone. Their wording is neutral on purpose (for example, "Take a minute for yourself today.") and never names your cycle.

We cannot see, open or recover this data. It is not sent to us, to Google, or to any other third party by Amavi, and it is never used for advertising.

Protection. Amavi adds no passcode, Face ID lock or extra encryption of its own. The data is protected by iOS (the app sandbox and the encryption of your iPhone's storage, which depends on your iPhone passcode). Anyone who can unlock your iPhone can open Amavi.

Backups. Amavi does not use iCloud or CloudKit. If you back up your iPhone (to iCloud or a computer), the backup can include Amavi's data, under your Apple Account's and backup's own settings.

Deleting it. In the app, Profile, Delete all data, then Delete everything removes your logs, profile and settings and resets reminders and widgets. If Apple Health sync is on at that moment, it also asks Health to remove the samples Amavi saved there. If you turned sync off earlier, the samples Amavi saved stay in Health: remove them in the Health app (Sharing, Apps, Amavi, Delete All Data). Deleting the app removes its data from your iPhone, but not what it saved in Apple Health.

5. Permissions

Amavi works without any of these. Change them any time in iOS Settings.

  • Tracking (iOS, App Tracking Transparency). Asked once, when you tap the button on the Welcome screen, after Google's consent form where that form applies. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, the advertising identifier is not shared and ads are not personalised with it; ads still appear.
  • Notifications. Asked only when you switch on a reminder. Local reminders scheduled on your iPhone; no push server is involved.
  • Apple Health (HealthKit, optional). Asked only when you switch on Profile, Apple Health, Sync with Apple Health. Amavi reads and writes the same six types: menstrual flow, spotting, ovulation test results, pregnancy test results, sexual activity and basal body temperature. It reads only what other apps saved (up to two years back) and writes what you log in Amavi. This happens on your iPhone through Apple's HealthKit; none of it is sent to us or to the ad service. Turn it off any time in Profile, Apple Health or in the Health app.

6. Consent, ads and tracking

Order on first launch: (1) while the splash plays, Google's consent form, where it is required (the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and some other places); (2) when you tap the button on the Welcome screen, the iOS tracking prompt; (3) then the app starts the ad SDK and asks for an ad. An ad is requested only if Google's consent status allows it. Until the form is answered, no ad is requested where the form applies.

If you decline personalised ads, you still get limited, non-personalised ads. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, ads still appear without the advertising identifier. To stop ads you would have to stop using the app; Amavi has no paid version.

Change your choice any time: in the app, Profile, Ad privacy choices (shown only where Google's consent form applies). On iOS, also Settings, Privacy & Security, Tracking.

Amavi has no analytics or crash-reporting service, so there is nothing else to consent to. The ad is never shown when you open the app from a reminder or a widget.

7. How long data is kept

Data on your device stays until you delete it in the app or delete the app. Data held by the services above is kept for the period listed in the table. We do not keep copies of our own. Emails you send us are kept as long as needed to answer, and at most 2 years.

8. Transfers outside the EEA

Google may process the ad-related data in the table in the United States or other countries. Transfers rely on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework for certified companies or on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (art. 45 and 46 GDPR). Your cycle and health data are not transferred anywhere by Amavi.

9. Your rights (EEA, UK, Switzerland)

Special category data. Cycle, period, symptom, sexual activity and pregnancy data are health data, a special category under art. 9 GDPR. We do not receive or process it: it exists only on your iPhone, under your control, and you decide what to log, whether to sync with Apple Health and when to delete it. Where Apple Health sync or the Health permission needs your agreement, you give it in the iOS permission sheet, and you can withdraw it in the Health app or iOS Settings at any time.

You have the right to access, correct and delete your data, to restrict or object to processing (including to object at any time to processing based on legitimate interest), to data portability, and to withdraw consent at any time without affecting earlier processing.

Because your logs exist only on your iPhone, we hold no copy: you see, correct and delete them in the app (Profile, Delete all data deletes everything). Amavi has no export button yet; if you use Apple Health sync, the Health app can export your health data (Health, your profile, Export All Health Data). For the ad data Google holds, use Profile, Ad privacy choices (shown only where Google's consent form applies). or Google's own tools, or write to iosallapps@gmail.com and we will pass the request on. We usually cannot tell which Google data is yours, so include your device's advertising ID if you can.

You can complain to the supervisory authority where you live or work, or to ours: Autoritatea Națională de Supraveghere a Prelucrării Datelor cu Caracter Personal (ANSPDCP), www.dataprotection.ro. The list of EU authorities: edpb.europa.eu.

10. United States

  • We do not sell personal information for money.
  • When you allow tracking and personalised ads, Google AdMob uses your advertising identifier for ads based on activity in other apps. Some US state laws (for example California) call this "sharing" or "targeted advertising". You can opt out at any time: refuse tracking on iOS (Settings, Privacy & Security, Tracking), or email us and we will treat it as an opt-out request.
  • Residents of California and other US states with privacy laws can ask to know, correct or delete their personal information, and will not be treated differently for doing so. The only personal information that leaves your iPhone is the ad data in the table above (identifiers, internet activity, approximate location from the IP address). Requests: iosallapps@gmail.com.

Consumer health data

This is the consumer health data privacy policy for Amavi, for Washington's My Health My Data Act, Nevada's SB 370, Connecticut's consumer health data provisions and similar laws in other US states. It adds to the sections above and changes none of them.

  • What we collect: nothing. We, the developer, do not collect, receive or store consumer health data. The cycle, period, symptom, sexual activity, pregnancy, temperature and similar data Amavi uses comes from what you enter and, if you turn it on, from Apple Health. It is processed only on your iPhone (see Data that stays on your device).
  • Why it is used: only to show you your logs, forecasts, insights, reminders and answers in the app, and, if you turn on Apple Health sync, to read and write the six Health types listed under Permissions.
  • Sharing: we do not share or sell consumer health data, and no affiliate or other third party receives any from us. Google's ad and consent services never receive what you log (see Consent, ads and tracking). The one health-related fact Google can infer is that an ad request came from a period tracking app (see Services that receive data); you can stop personalised use of your advertising identifier by refusing tracking.
  • Consent: we do not collect your data, so there is nothing for you to consent to or revoke with us. Your permission for Apple Health is given and withdrawn in the iOS permission sheet, the Health app and Settings, Privacy & Security, Health, Amavi.
  • Your rights: you can see and delete this data on your iPhone (Deleting it), withdraw Health access at any time, and email iosallapps@gmail.com for any other request. If we decline a request, you can appeal by replying to our answer, and we will respond within 45 days. We will never use geofencing around health facilities.

11. Children

Amavi is not designed for children. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13, or under the age of digital consent in their country (13 to 16 in the EU), and we hold no data of anyone, because your logs stay on your iPhone. If you believe a child gave us data, write to iosallapps@gmail.com.

Setup asks for your birth date, which stays on your iPhone. It decides how ads are requested for people under 18 and under 16 (see Services that receive data). Amavi does not block younger users.

12. Security

Your logs never travel over a network: Amavi has no server and no code that uploads them. The ad SDK connects to Google over the network, as described above. On the device, data is protected by iOS (app sandbox and storage encryption). Amavi adds no passcode, Face ID lock or extra encryption of its own, so protect your iPhone with a passcode, and remember that Lock Screen widgets and notifications are visible on a locked phone (reminders are worded neutrally). No system is perfectly secure, so we keep what leaves your iPhone to the minimum listed here.

13. This website

https://amaviapp.com is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA), which records the IP address of visitors in server logs for security. The site sets no cookies and uses no analytics or ads. It stores only your language choice in your browser (local storage).

14. Changes

If the app starts handling data differently, we update this page and the date at the top before the new version ships. Important changes are also mentioned in the store update notes.
